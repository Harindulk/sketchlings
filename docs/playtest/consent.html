Sketchlings

Permission for your child to play and be shown

Hello! I'm Harindu, a solo game developer at StarFun Games in Sri Lanka. I made Sketchlings, a TV game for Amazon's "Build, Ship, Shape" developer hackathon, an online contest for app makers. Children draw a creature on paper, a phone takes a photo of it, and the creature comes alive on the TV with a name, a voice and a special power. Then everyone races the creatures, using phones as controllers.

What will happen

On Friday 23 October 2026, about 1 to 3 pm, at , teachers I know run a short session with 3 or 4 children aged 4 to 10. Your child draws a creature, snaps it with one of my phones and races it on a TV, for about an hour with breaks. I watch and write down what works and what doesn't. It is a test of the game, not of your child, and they can stop at any time.

What may be shown

Your child's drawing, the creature made from it, and their first name and age (for example "Nila, 6"):

  • on the TV during the game;
  • in a public YouTube video, a demo of under 3 minutes that stays online after the contest;
  • on the contest entry on Devpost, which Amazon's judges and the public can see.

Never a surname, school, address or anything that says where you live.

Filming

The camera films hands, phones, the paper and the TV screen only. It also records sound. Your child's face is shown only if you tick "Face" below, and their voice only if you tick "Voice"; otherwise I cut or mute it in the video. In my written results (on GitHub and Devpost) your child is "a 6-year-old", never named.

Nothing else

No accounts and no apps: your child uses my phones. The "shout" button only measures how loud a roar is; the sound is not recorded or sent. I collect nothing else about your child.

Drawings and AI

To make the creature, a picture of the drawing goes to Claude, an AI service by Anthropic (a US company), which suggests its name, personality and power. Only the drawing is sent: the phone cuts it out of the photo first. Your child's name, age, face and voice are never sent to the AI. The phones reach my computer through a secure link run by Cloudflare. The creature's voice is made on my own computer.

If you tick No for AI, I switch the AI off for the whole session: my computer makes every creature itself (simpler names), and your child plays the same.

What I keep

The paper drawing goes home with your child. The cut-out, the creature and the first name stay in the game's "Hall of Fame" on my computer, never in the public code. My notes and any footage I don't use stay private. I delete them by 31 December 2026, except what is in the video and the written results. Ask me to delete anything sooner.

Saying no, or changing your mind

It is your choice, and your child's: I only include children who are happy to play. To withdraw, tell the teacher or me (), no reason needed, any time before the video goes online (planned for the evening of Friday 23 October 2026), and I leave your child out. After that I will edit or remove the video and the entry as far as the contest allows, but I can't pull back copies other people have saved.

Sketchlings

Trying the game first: adult tester OK

Hello! I'm Harindu, a solo game developer at StarFun Games in Sri Lanka. Sketchlings is a TV game for Amazon's "Build, Ship, Shape" hackathon: you draw a creature, a phone brings it to life on the TV, and everyone races the creatures with their phones. Before the children play, I want to see whether an adult who has never seen it can set it up alone.

What will happen

About 10 minutes, before the children's session. A laptop stands in for the TV. I give you one instruction and then stay quiet: I time each step and write down where you get stuck, what you ask, and your answers to two questions about your creature. It is a test of the game, not of you. You can stop at any time. Afterwards I explain the children's session.

What I publish

The written results, on GitHub and on the hackathon entry on Devpost, as "Tester 1", "Tester 2" and so on: times, what was hard, and maybe your exact words. Never your name.

Filming (your choice below)

Hands, phones and the screen only, never your face. If you say yes, it may appear in the public demo video (YouTube and Devpost). This covers the children's session too.

Phones, drawings and AI

  • If you use your own phone, it opens a web page; nothing is installed. The first name you type (any name is fine) shows on the screen with your creature, stays in the game's "Hall of Fame" on my computer, and may appear in the video when the game is on screen.
  • Drawings you snap are sent to Claude by Anthropic for the creature's name, personality and power, unless the AI is off for the session. Your name is not sent.
  • The phone reaches my computer through a temporary secure link run by Cloudflare.
  • The ROAR microphone only checks how loud it is. The sound is not recorded or sent anywhere.

What I keep

My notes and any footage I don't use stay private, and I will delete them by 31 December 2026. You can withdraw any time before the video goes online (planned for the evening of Friday 23 October 2026) and I will leave you out. After that I will remove what I can.
